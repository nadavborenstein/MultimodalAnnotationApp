#### Step 1: Identify Claims  

1. Carefully examine the Tweet text and the Image.  
2. Determine whether the Tweet text and/or Image makes a claim.
3. **Understand** the claim made by the Tweet’s text and/or image.

**What is a claim?**
- **Claim**: A statement that asserts something about reality, which can, in principle, be evaluated as true or false using evidence, reasoning, or authoritative sources.  For example, the statement: “Red meat is good for your health”. 
- **Not a claim**: Personal opinions, jokes, advertisements, or content that does not contain any verifiable statement.  For example, the preposition: “I really like red meat”.

Then, answer the question “Does the tweet and/or image make a claim?”. If answered "No", briefly explain your choice. If answered "Yes", briefly describe the claim in your own words. 

⚠️ Claims can be either explicit (e.g., the tweet’s text stating “16 billion passwords have been leaked from Google yesterday”) or implicit (e.g., the tweet’s text is “look at this mess!”, and the image is a photo of a protest. In this case, the implicit claim is “this protest took place, and was messy”.). 

⚠️ The claims can be made either by the tweet’s text or by the image. Even if the Tweet’s text (💬) is empty, the image (🖼️) may still contain a claim.


---

#### Step 2: Analyze Misleading Claims  

If the tweet or image **does make a claim**:  

1. Read the *Additional context 💡* and understand **why the claim is misleading**. 
2. Answer a short series of **4–8 questions** about the image, the tweet’s text, and their relationship.


Each question will include:  
- A **Yes/No question**. Each question will also contain a short text explaining the question.  
- A **free-text field** to justify your choice (mandatory or optional, depending on the question).  
- A **“Confirm” button** to move forward to the next question. ⚠️ Once you press “Confirm,” you cannot go back and change your answer.
